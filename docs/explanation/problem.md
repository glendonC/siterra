# The problem

Siterra exists because decisions about rangelands need careful analysis, and free satellite data does not supply it. This page sets out the facts behind that claim. Each figure keeps its source's wording and scope, and each source ID links to an entry in the [science source register](../../research/science/source-register.md). Labels follow the [documentation index](../README.md#evidence-labels): **[V]** checked on the cited page on 2026-10-26, **[H]** a framing or hypothesis, **[U]** not verified.

## Rangelands and the people who depend on them

Rangelands cover more than half of Earth's land and support the livelihoods of about two billion people (SCI-125, SCI-127) [V]. FAO estimates that roughly half of them may be degraded; other assessments put the share with declining trends near 20 per cent and warn that this may be an underestimate (SCI-126, SCI-127) [V]. The UN declared 2026 the International Year of Rangelands and Pastoralists and asked governments, researchers and companies to fill the gaps in what is known about them (SCI-125) [V].

## Free data, scarce analysis

Sentinel-2 imagery is free under EU law, and when Landsat became free in 2008 its use rose twentyfold (SCI-18, SCI-134) [V]. Even so, the IPBES land assessment found that remote sensing and open data are not commonly used in land decisions, and that most decision tools describe the state of the land without the people who use it (SCI-129) [V]. The UNCCD adds that rangeland monitoring is expensive and that project teams often lack the capacity to do it (SCI-127) [V]. The gap between free data and a defensible decision about one particular place is the problem Siterra addresses [H].

## What a satellite record cannot tell

Greenness follows rainfall closely. In simulations, a vegetation-index trend only became statistically detectable once 30 to 40 per cent of the vegetation had been degraded (SCI-29) [V]. In a region that has been through repeated droughts, a greener or browner year therefore says little about grazing on its own.

Morocco's 2024 cereal harvest was more than 40 per cent below average, and the government distributed subsidized barley to livestock farmers in the worst-hit regions (SCI-25, SCI-44) [V]. These are national figures; they set the context and are never scaled down to one valley. On the southern slopes of the Central High Atlas, studies describe herders settling near villages as droughts recur, which concentrates grazing close to settlements (SCI-104, SCI-132) [V]. In Aït Bouguemez, a published synthesis reports that many herders have stopped winter transhumance (SCI-19) [V].

## Who decides

Authority over these pastures is layered. Law 113-13 of 2016 and its 2018 decrees created transhumance permits issued by the agriculture department and a national rangeland commission with regional committees (SCI-131) [V]; how they apply in Tabant has not been checked [U]. Villages govern access to their pastures through agdal rules with their own opening dates (SCI-01, SCI-19 to SCI-21, SCI-23, SCI-24, SCI-96, SCI-97, SCI-102) [V]. Software represents neither of them. ANDZOA, a national agency sometimes cited for the region, works only on argan and oasis zones and is not cited for this case (SCI-141) [V].

## Claims this case does not make

Siterra claims no saving in time or cost. No source measures how long a rangeland scoping study takes in Morocco or a comparable setting, and the nearest published figure, for EU statutory impact assessments, describes a different instrument (SCI-135). It also gives no headcount of pastoralists, because published estimates range from 200 million to 500 million depending on how pastoralism is defined (SCI-130, SCI-127).

## Figures we do not use

These claims circulate widely but are weak, unverified or misquoted. The right-hand column gives the wording Siterra uses instead.

| Claim often seen | Problem | Say instead |
|---|---|---|
| Half of the world's rangelands are degraded | An upper-bound estimate | "FAO estimates roughly half may be degraded" |
| Rangelands are 54% of Earth's surface | The scope is land, and definitions vary | "More than half of Earth's land" (SCI-125) or "over 54 per cent of the terrestrial surface" (SCI-127) |
| 500 million pastoralists | Sources range from 200 to 500 million | "About two billion people's livelihoods depend on rangelands" |
| 40% of the world's land is degraded | Most assessments say 20 to 40% degraded or degrading | "Between 20 and 40 per cent of land is degraded or degrading" (SCI-128) |
| Land degradation threatens USD 44 trillion of output | That output relies on natural capital; it is not threatened by degradation | Do not use |
| 75% of Earth's land is degraded | IPBES says less than a quarter of land is free from substantial human impact; impact is not degradation | Do not use |
| 3.2 billion people live on degraded land | IPBES says their well-being is negatively impacted | Use the IPBES scope (fact 4) |
| Morocco's herd fell 38% since 2016 | Reported in media only; not found on an official page | Use SCI-25 and SCI-44 |
| Morocco has 53 million hectares of rangeland | The primary paper was not accessible | Do not use until the primary is read [U] |
| Any cost or duration for "an environmental study" | No comparable source | Do not use |
