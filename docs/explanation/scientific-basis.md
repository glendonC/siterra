# Scientific basis and limits

Sources are indexed in the [science bibliography](../../research/science/source-register.md). Validated observation and recipe bundles for the Aït Bouguemez catchment are cataloged in [`data/bundles/catalog.json`](../../data/bundles/catalog.json).

## Decision and scope

Proceed with a **pasture assessment and field-validation investigation**, with Aït Bouguemez in Morocco's Central High Atlas as the named geographic context. Do not launch a case titled “overgrazing detected.” The useful deliverable is a sourced comparison of possible explanations, followed by a reviewable monitoring and feasibility package. Satellite work is an observation layer within that investigation.

**Verified context:** published field research establishes communal resource governance and pastoral livelihoods in this area. It does not establish today's condition of a newly selected slope. Romagny, Auclair and Elgueroua describe customary resource institutions and changing institutional relationships in Aït Bouguemez [SCI-01](https://horizon.documentation.ird.fr/exl-doc/pleins_textes/2021-05/010043415.pdf). Agdal is an institution governing access and use; it should not be reduced to an AI-prescribed fence or a universal closure calendar.

A regional study based on interviews with 50 farmers across three Azilal municipalities links changes in pastoral systems to climate, resource availability and mobility. It describes different trajectories at different elevations. This is historical, interview-based regional evidence, not a stocking-rate measurement for the demonstration area [SCI-02](https://doi.org/10.1071/RJ21039).

A 2026 review synthesizes climate and socioeconomic pressures and identifies herd mobility, collective action and crop-livestock integration as relevant adaptation themes. It is a review, with underlying studies of varying scope; its numerical examples must not become current local household estimates [SCI-03](https://doi.org/10.1186/s42055-026-00126-4).

The valley lies in the rural commune of Tabant (Province of Azilal), runs about 40 km east to west with a floor at 1800 to 2200 m below ranges above 3000 m, and holds more than thirty villages; village forest agdals cover roughly 20 to 200 ha each with opening dates set by village assemblies [SCI-19](https://doi.org/10.5751/ES-04429-160424). The 2024 census gives the commune a legal population of 16,401 in 2,910 households; this describes the whole commune, not herding households or the catchment [SCI-35](https://www.hcp.ma/Population-legale-du-Royaume-du-Maroc-repartie-par-regions-provinces-et-prefectures-et-communes-selon-les-resultats-du_a3974.html). Remote-sensing history exists for the valley's **forests**, not its pastures: aerial photographs from 1964 compared with 2002 SPOT imagery, field-validated, show canopy maintained or improved under agdal control and degradation outside it [SCI-20, SCI-21](../../research/science/source-register.md). Treat this as evidence that customary institutions can matter, not as a pasture result. High-pasture agdal studies elsewhere in the High Atlas stress religious, economic and social dimensions alongside ecology [SCI-23, SCI-24](../../research/science/source-register.md); the multi-chapter Agdal volume is a pointer only [SCI-22](../../research/science/source-register.md).

A 2026 participatory field study in three Aït Bouguemez villages documents women's landscape knowledge and vegetation surveys. It supports including people whose knowledge a herder-only consultation would miss. Published village coordinates provide a geographic locator, not pasture boundaries. Confidential discussion transcripts are explicitly unavailable [SCI-04](https://doi.org/10.1002/pan3.70379).

## Evidence language required in the product

| Label | Meaning | Example acceptable before processing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Observation | Actual measured value, acquisition window, geometry, unit, valid coverage and method | “No observation available yet.” |
| Published context | A finding from another study, with place, date and method | “Regional interviews described changing herd mobility.” |
| Hypothesis | A candidate explanation requiring discriminating evidence | “Rainfall variation could contribute to observed greenness variation.” |
| Model estimate | A named quantitative model, inputs, validation and uncertainty | Not available for biomass or carrying capacity in this MVP. |
| Scenario | Conditional management option; assumptions visible | “A negotiated rest period would require agreed access rules.” |
| Required validation | Evidence needed before a decision | Field vegetation surveys and locally verified management records. |
| Verified outcome | Documented post-action result against a defined baseline | Unavailable; never manufacture an outcome for the demonstration. |

NDVI uses red and near-infrared reflectance as a greenness proxy. Climate, topography, soils and management can affect this signal; spatial association alone does not identify a cause. USGS explains these interpretation limits in a different regional context; it is methodological context, not Atlas evidence [SCI-05](https://www.usgs.gov/centers/eros/science/land-productivity). UNCCD combines land cover, productivity and soil carbon in land-degradation reporting; a short Sentinel series is not an official SDG 15.3.1 assessment [SCI-06](https://www.unccd.int/resources/manuals-and-guides/good-practice-guidance-sdg-indicator-1531-proportion-land-degraded).

## Competing explanations and useful next work

| Hypothesis | What remote data can contribute | What is still required |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rainfall variability | Same-month vegetation and regional rainfall anomalies; prespecified rainfall lags. National context: drought in spring 2019 and 2020, recovery in 2021, a well-below-average 2022 season (FAO GIEWS, SCI-25; national, not local) | Local precipitation records, snow timing, temperature and field interpretation; a climate-corrected productivity analysis over a longer record |
| Grazing-related pressure | Spatial patterns and persistent changes worth checking | Animal numbers by species, residence time, access routes, pasture area, seasonal movements and management history |
| Phenology, snow or terrain | Observation dates, snow masks, elevation/aspect strata and image inspection. Atlas snow cover varies strongly between years and its melt feeds rivers and recharge (SCI-26, SCI-27); Sentinel-2 snow observations are usable in High Atlas terrain (SCI-28) | Local seasonal calendar, shadow/snow quality checks |
| Land-use or composition change | Context land-cover strata and visible imagery; a 2026 valley study reports apple growing and tourism alongside smaller, more sedentary herds, which makes valley-floor orchards a likely confounder for greening (SCI-105) | Crop, orchard and irrigation history; whether orchards expanded during 2019 to 2025 and whether the 2021 land-cover mask captures them; botanical composition (greening can include unpalatable plants) |
| Observation artefact | Clear-observation counts, coverage, source scenes and mask sensitivity | Independent manual inspection and repeat processing |

These hypotheses can coexist. Do not force a binary drought-versus-grazing answer. Rainfall-adjusted residuals also contain omitted variables; they are not a “grazing impact” estimate. In simulation, 30 to 40% degradation was needed before vegetation-index trends became detectable under rainfall-driven variability, and residual-trend methods broke down under some conditions [SCI-29](../../research/science/source-register.md); rain-use efficiency can stay correlated with rainfall and fail to normalize it [SCI-31](../../research/science/source-register.md). Segmented residual-trend methods exist [SCI-30](../../research/science/source-register.md). In sparse cover, soil background affects NDVI; MSAVI may be computed as an optional sensitivity check only, never as a second headline index [SCI-32, SCI-33](../../research/science/source-register.md).

Standardized climate-corrected productivity methods exist [D]: Trends.Earth implements RESTREND, rain-use efficiency and water-use efficiency corrections with CHIRPS inputs for SDG 15.3.1 reporting, and its documentation notes that productivity declines may reflect rainfall ([SCI-34](../../research/science/source-register.md)). Siterra precomputes both descriptive short-record comparisons (`monthly_departure`, `aligned_antecedent` over 0 to 3 month antecedent windows) and 26-season MODIS long-record analyses (`restrend_long_record`, `pixel_trend_long_record`, `season_trend_long_record`, and `snow_cover_by_band` over 2000 to 2025). When the rainfall slope confidence interval in `restrend_long_record` includes zero, the result is marked `precondition_not_met` and no rainfall-adjusted residual trend is claimed.

Hypothesis status words in data and UI: not yet tested, consistent with available evidence, contradicted by available evidence, cannot be assessed with current data, needs reassessment ([Data contracts](../reference/data-contracts.md#hypothesis-status)). Several hypotheses can be consistent at once; none is ranked by a score.

## Why satellite greenness can rise while pastoral forage stagnates or declines

Over the 2000 to 2025 Terra MODIS record (`restrend_long_record`, `pixel_trend_long_record` and `season_trend_long_record`), unadjusted growing-season NDVI trends upward in the catchment and across elevation and aspect strata in both spring (March to June) and summer (June to September). The corpus documents three mechanisms showing why upward satellite greenness does not mean improved pasture forage:

1. **Unpalatable xerophytic shrubs and species shifts under grazing pressure [V]:** In Aït Bouguemez, upper rangelands above the forest belt are "mainly composed of spiny xerophytic vegetation and reduced areas of short grasslands (*Almu*)" ([SCI-19](../../research/science/source-register.md), p. 2). Across Central High Atlas pastures in Azilal province, herders report the disappearance of palatable species (*Astragalus armatus*, *Thymus satureioides*) and the spread of unpalatable plants (*Thymelaea virgata*, *Euphorbia nicaeensis*) under recurrent droughts and grazing pressure ([SCI-03](../../research/science/source-register.md), pp. 7 to 8). Classic High Atlas montane cushion xerophytes (*Bupleurum spinosum*, *Alyssum spinosum*, *Erinacea anthyllis*, *Cytisus balansae*) form dense perennial woody canopies that contribute positive red-to-NIR reflectance contrast while offering poor or inaccessible small-ruminant forage [H for their exact 2000 to 2025 trend in this catchment; requires botanical transect verification]. Furthermore, local drone calibration over 180 destructively sampled plots in Midelt and Aït Bouguemez showed that the best spectral index (RVI) explained only 42% of herbaceous biomass variance in cross-validation and bare soil masked biomass below about 800 kg/ha ([SCI-106](../../research/science/source-register.md)).
2. **Valley-floor apple orchards and irrigated fodder expansion [V]:** Cultivated land on the valley floor (1800 to 2200 m) is about 80% irrigated via surface-water channels (*seguia*); orchards promoted since the mid-1990s (mainly apple, with some walnut) occupy around 10% of total agricultural area, often with irrigated alfalfa (*Almou*) grown beneath the trees ([SCI-03](../../research/science/source-register.md), pp. 5, 7, 9 to 10; [SCI-19](../../research/science/source-register.md), pp. 2, 9; [SCI-105](../../research/science/source-register.md)). In `season_trend_long_record`, the strongest summer (June to September) NDVI increases occur on level ground (+0.0317 NDVI per decade), WorldCover cropland/built-up (+0.0494 per decade) and WorldCover tree cover (+0.0268 per decade), confirming that irrigated valley-floor agriculture and tree crops strongly lift catchment-wide greenness.
3. **Terra MODIS orbital drift and solar illumination geometry [V]:** Terra's last inclination maneuver took place in February 2020, and its mean local equator crossing drifted from 10:30 to about 9:06 by December 2025 ([SCI-159](../../research/science/source-register.md)). Earlier morning overpasses view steep mountain slopes at lower solar elevation with longer shadows and altered bidirectional reflectance, which can shift composite NDVI in later seasons without any change in vegetation on the ground.

## Scientific review of field-check pairs and the `merge@2` three-period rule

### Field-check pairs for separating competing explanations

When satellite observations leave multiple explanations open, the branch merge (`DF-09`) selects a minimum set cover of field checks over the remaining open explanation pairs. Both the case configuration's five high-level field checks (`packages/domain/src/cases/case_aitbouguemez.json`) and their concrete field-protocol counterparts are evaluated below:

| Field check (case config / field protocol) | Claimed explanation pairs separated | Scientific evaluation and required condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `local_rain_records` / `snowmelt_timing_loggers` (in-valley precipitation gauges, temperature/snowmelt timing loggers, shadow/snow QA) | `rainfall` vs `grazing`, `land_use`, `snow_terrain`, `data_artefact`; specifically `snow_terrain` vs `rainfall` and `grazing` | **Valid under condition [D, V: SCI-26, SCI-27]:** CHIRPS 5 km grid cells ([SCI-09](../../research/science/source-register.md)) cannot resolve valley-scale rain shadows or distinguish rain from snow storage. Paired in-valley rain gauges and elevation-banded temperature or snowmelt loggers separate snowmelt-timing shifts (`snow_terrain`) from wet-season rainfall deficits (`rainfall`) and grazing pressure (`grazing`), provided loggers are placed across the three elevation bands (`elevation_low`, `elevation_mid`, `elevation_high`) and paired with green-up dates. |
| `herd_records` / `exclosure_pair_cover` and `herd_calendar_participatory` (paired grazed/ungrazed exclosure plots and participatory herd-movement calendars) | `grazing` vs `rainfall`, `land_use`, `snow_terrain`, `data_artefact` | **Valid under BACI / paired-plot condition [V: SCI-87, SCI-90, SCI-93]:** Participatory herd records (`herd_records` / `herd_calendar_participatory`) establish whether animal residence time and opening dates changed ([SCI-03](../../research/science/source-register.md), [SCI-19](../../research/science/source-register.md)), separating `grazing` from `land_use` or `data_artefact`, but by themselves cannot separate `grazing` from `rainfall` when drought and stocking co-vary. Separating `grazing` from `rainfall` on the ground requires paired browsed versus unbrowsed exclosure plots (`exclosure_pair_cover`) within the same elevation and aspect stratum so both plots experience identical weather ([SCI-93](../../research/science/source-register.md), [SCI-90](../../research/science/source-register.md)). |
| `orchard_history` / `orchard_boundary_ground_truth` and `species_palatability_transect` (2019 to 2025 parcel/irrigation mapping and botanical palatability transects) | `land_use` (and composition shift) vs `rainfall`, `grazing`, `snow_terrain`, `data_artefact` | **Valid and essential [V: SCI-03, SCI-13, SCI-105, SCI-106]:** Because ESA WorldCover 2021 ([SCI-13](../../research/science/source-register.md)) is a static single-year snapshot that maps only 1.1% of the catchment as cropland and groups valley-floor orchards into tree cover, ground-truthing orchard and irrigation boundaries (`orchard_boundary_ground_truth`) separates agricultural conversion (`land_use`) from rangeland change. Botanical transects recording palatable herbs (*Astragalus armatus*, *Thymus satureioides*) versus unpalatable or spiny xerophytes (*Thymelaea virgata*, *Euphorbia nicaeensis*, cushion shrubs; `species_palatability_transect`) are required to separate genuine forage recovery from unpalatable shrub expansion ([SCI-03](../../research/science/source-register.md), pp. 7 to 8; [SCI-19](../../research/science/source-register.md), p. 2). |
| `seasonal_calendar` (customary agdal opening/closing calendar and snow/shadow timing check) | `snow_terrain` vs `rainfall`, `grazing`, `land_use`, `data_artefact` | **Valid with stratum-resolved dates [V: SCI-03, SCI-19, SCI-96]:** Because village assemblies adjust agdal opening dates in response to snow and forage conditions ([SCI-19](../../research/science/source-register.md), pp. 6 to 9, Table 2; [SCI-03](../../research/science/source-register.md), p. 12), recording the actual annual opening and closing dates per pasture unit separates phenological or snow-delayed green-up (`snow_terrain`) from grazing off-take (`grazing`). |
| `scene_inspection` (independent manual inspection of source scenes, SCL masks and baseline harmonization) | `data_artefact` vs `rainfall`, `grazing`, `land_use`, `snow_terrain` | **Valid and deterministic [V: SCI-07, SCI-159]:** Checking individual Sentinel-2 L2A scenes for unmasked cloud fringes, terrain shadow on north-facing slopes, the January 2022 processing-baseline DN shift ([SCI-07](../../research/science/source-register.md)), and Terra MODIS post-2020 orbital drift ([SCI-159](../../research/science/source-register.md)) directly separates sensor/processing artefacts (`data_artefact`) from all four ground processes. |

**Structural note on star-shaped `separates` declarations (`case_aitbouguemez.json`):** In `case_aitbouguemez.json`, each of the 5 field checks currently lists all 4 pairs involving its own explanation (a star graph on 5 nodes). Mathematically, any single star covers only 4 of the \(\binom{5}{2} = 10\) unordered pairs; a minimum test cover over all 5 open explanations requires \(5 - 1 = 4\) checks (since 3 stars cover at most \(4 + 3 + 2 = 9 < 10\) edges). While `scene_inspection` genuinely separates `data_artefact` from all four biophysical explanations, single-domain biophysical checks (`local_rain_records`, `herd_records`, `orchard_history`, `seasonal_calendar`) only separate their own driver from another biophysical driver when paired with vegetation response in the same stratum (for example, `exclosure_pair_cover` or `species_palatability_transect`).

### Why `minPeriodsForNecessarySign = 3` (`DEFAULT_MIN_SIGN_PERIODS`) in `merge@2` is a reasonable heuristic floor

In `packages/domain/src/branches.ts`, `merge@2` sets `DEFAULT_MIN_SIGN_PERIODS = 3` (`minPeriodsForNecessarySign: 3`), so a necessary `departure_sign` prediction that fails over only 1 or 2 monthly periods results in `cannot_be_assessed` (flagged as `inadequate_data`) rather than immediately marking the explanation `contradicted`.

1. **Protection against single-month cloud, snow and compositing noise [V, A]:** In the Sentinel-2 monthly series (`coverage_summary` and `monthly_departure`), a pixel-month is accepted with as few as 3 clear dates, and Sen2Cor scene classification (`SCL` classes 4 and 5) can pass sub-pixel cloud edges, snow fringes and terrain shadows ([SCI-07](../../research/science/source-register.md)). Moreover, against a 7-year reference (or 6 other years in leave-one-year-out), the sample median has high variance in months with transitional snowmelt or erratic spring showers ([SCI-26](../../research/science/source-register.md), [SCI-48](../../research/science/source-register.md)). Under a symmetric null where a noisy month has probability \(p = 0.5\) of showing the opposite departure sign by chance, contradicting a necessary prediction on \(k = 1\) month has a 50% false-rejection rate, and \(k = 2\) months still has a \(0.5^2 = 25\%\) false-rejection rate; requiring at least \(k = 3\) evaluated periods reduces the independent coin-flip false-contradiction rate to \(0.5^3 = 12.5\%\) (and spans a full three-month seasonal quarter, matching the 3-month antecedent rainfall window and the 3-month spring agdal closure in [SCI-96](../../research/science/source-register.md)).
2. **Heuristic decision rule `[D]`, not a formal statistical test:** Monthly NDVI departures exhibit positive lag-1 serial correlation from soil moisture and canopy memory ([SCI-46](../../research/science/source-register.md), [SCI-48](../../research/science/source-register.md)), so three consecutive months are not independent Bernoulli trials and \(0.5^3 = 0.125\) is not a calibrated \(p\)-value. Accordingly, `DEFAULT_MIN_SIGN_PERIODS = 3` must be documented as a conservative engineering floor `[D]` against 1- to 2-month satellite noise, never as a statistical significance threshold.

## Analysis and forecasting

The model chooses and interprets deterministic analysis recipes and never computes; Siterra forecasts nothing until a pre-registered backtest shows skill ([analysis recipes](../reference/analysis-recipes.md)).

## Interventions: compare conditions, not invented benefits

The P0 comparison contains three options: (1) participatory monitoring before changing access, (2) community-negotiated seasonal rest/access adjustments where governance and forage needs support them, and (3) mobility/feed-contingency feasibility work where access, cost and distributional effects can be established. These are **design choices informed by context**, not locally endorsed prescriptions or a ranked treatment recommendation.

For each, show ecological rationale, household exposure, authority, evidence gap, reversibility and conditions that would rule it out. National livestock totals exist (2025 count, SCI-44) but no provincial or commune figures were found; never downscale national numbers to the valley. Include who loses access, seasonal feed alternatives, cash and labor burdens, water access and displacement of grazing elsewhere. No unverified currency estimates, household counts, jobs, recovery dates or percentage improvements.

A field package should request locally agreed management units; representative vegetation transects or quadrats covering elevation, aspect and management contrasts; species/palatability and bare-ground observations; repeat fixed photographs with consent; documented livestock-use calendars; and livelihood interviews designed with community representatives. A qualified local team must choose sampling intensity, consent process, languages, governance representation and season. Do not publish household identities, sensitive access disputes or precise sensitive sites by default.

## Prohibited claims

- “Overgrazing detected,” “pasture degraded by livestock,” or causal attribution from an NDVI decline alone.
- Biomass, livestock carrying capacity, animal units per hectare or biodiversity measured from uncalibrated NDVI. A 2026 drone calibration study with 180 destructively sampled plots in Aït Bouguemez and Midelt found the best index explained 42% of biomass variance and bare soil hid biomass below about 800 kg per hectare (SCI-106).
- "Significant trend" or any significance claim from a seven-year record (SCI-48), and any forecast, projection or outlook ([forecasting policy](../reference/analysis-recipes.md#forecasting-policy)).
- A 5 km rainfall product represented as 10 m rainfall or a pasture-specific weather station.
- “Agdal works everywhere,” locally approved closure dates, or verified rights/consent inferred from literature.
- Restoration success, measured community benefit, real partnerships, or calibrated forecasts that do not exist.
- Statistical confidence manufactured by treating correlated pixels or repeated monthly values as independent samples.

## Release gate

A public finding requires a reproducible input manifest, a verified analysis geometry and status, matching chart/map periods, quality coverage, a human-reviewed interpretation and citations. The P0 analysis extent is the valley catchment (a hydrological unit, [Data sources and reproducible methods](../reference/data-sources-and-methods.md#study-area)); it is never presented as a pasture or management boundary, and the field package asks for locally agreed management units. That is an honest completed investigation task; it is not a pasture diagnosis. See [study-area status](../../research/geospatial/study-area.md) and [methods](../reference/data-sources-and-methods.md).
