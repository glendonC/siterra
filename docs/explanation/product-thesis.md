# Product thesis

What Siterra is, who it serves, how its case discipline works, and the boundaries it enforces. Labels follow the [documentation index](../README.md#evidence-labels): **[V]** verified from a cited source, **[D]** design rule, **[H]** hypothesis, **[U]** unverified.

## Core design

Siterra executes an environmental case end to end: a commissioned investigation that produces a reviewable field-verification plan [D]. The system performs literature research, reproducible satellite and climate measurement, hypothesis testing against pre-registered predictions, and field-plan preparation inside strict authority boundaries. It never performs external outreach, spending, or land-management execution.

## The problem it answers

Free satellite and rainfall data has not made rangeland decisions easier, because the analysis that turns raw observations into a defensible decision about one specific watershed is specialized and rare where it is most needed [H]. Rangelands cover more than half of Earth's land and support about two billion people, and 2026 is the UN International Year of Rangelands and Pastoralists [V]. The cited facts and Moroccan context are detailed in [the problem](problem.md).

## What Siterra is

When an investigation is commissioned, Siterra plans a bounded task graph, retrieves and checks primary sources, loads reproducible satellite and climate observations for the frozen study geometry, tests competing explanations against pre-registered predictions, extracts livelihood and governance constraints, screens conditional response options, and prepares a field-verification plan with role-specific review requests. Every statement links to its evidence or measurement record, and every operational action stops at `prepared` until an authorized human reviewer decides. In environmental consulting terms, the output corresponds to the scoping and baseline phase of an engagement: a desk review, a remote-sensing baseline, a screening of options, and terms of reference for fieldwork [D].

Siterra investigates changes in land, water, and vegetation and the land-use practices that could explain them. It refuses questions that treat people as the explanation because of who they are (ethnicity, race, religion, nationality, migration status), and it does not investigate named private individuals (`SA-10`). Herders, herding households, grazing practices, and customary institutions (`agdal`) are in scope, as are verbatim quotations from published sources [D].

Siterra executes the analytical and preparation work and attributes all system actions to Siterra directly ("Siterra retrieved 6 sources"), never to simulated personas or fictional staff, while human reviewers retain sole authority over real-world decisions ([review and authority](../reference/review-and-authority.md)).

## Who it is for

The primary user is an environmental program manager, restoration lead, or agricultural development officer who must decide whether and how to act on a reported land-use concern without dedicated GIS or remote-sensing staff. The second role is a local initiator in the study region who needs a transparent, citation-backed account of what the satellite and documentary record shows, what remains uncertain, and what fieldwork would verify ([users and flows](users-and-flows.md#second-role-local-initiator)).

## Why case discipline matters

General-purpose assistants and standalone geospatial dashboards rarely maintain a single revision-controlled case record in which competing explanations remain open until measurements separate them, livelihood and governance unknowns block premature conclusions, reviews bind to exact entity revisions, updated inputs automatically mark dependent findings `stale`, and unresolved causes are converted into a minimum-cover field-measurement plan (`DF-01` to `DF-09`).

## What the user sees and does

Siterra produces a task plan, retrieved sources with page locators and verbatim excerpts, summarized observations with valid-pixel coverage, an explanation-branch ledger (`ExplanationBranch` and `BranchMerge`), livelihood and governance constraints, conditional response options, a stratified field-verification plan, review requests, and a decision brief.

The user confirms the analysis area, challenges interpretations, inspects source and measurement lineage, and records role-scoped review decisions. Every statement carries its claim kind (`observation`, `scientific_context`, `hypothesis`, `model_estimate`, `scenario`, `field_requirement`) and opens to its source, geometry, period, method, and coverage. Numbers are computed deterministically by code from checksummed bundles, never generated by a model.

Siterra refuses to output an overgrazing diagnosis from vegetation indices alone, uncalibrated biomass or carrying-capacity estimates, causal percentages, unvalidated forecasts, household headcounts, or fabricated consent claims.

## Generalizing the method across domains

Each domain requires its own pre-registered recipes, primary corpus, validation thresholds, and governance mapping; the rules on [comparable places](comparable-places.md) and [forecasting](../reference/analysis-recipes.md#forecasting-policy) apply across all cases:

| Domain | Verified need [V] | Required domain configuration |
|---|---|---|
| Post-wildfire recovery | The frequency of the most energetic wildfire events increased 2.2-fold from 2003 to 2023 (SCI-138) | Burn-severity recipes, fire perimeters, and field plot calibration |
| Land-degradation reporting | Assessments place 20 to 40% of land as degraded or degrading (SCI-128) | SDG 15.3.1 sub-indicators following UNCCD Good Practice Guidance (SCI-06) |
| Restoration and carbon project audits | Across 26 avoided-deforestation project sites, most did not significantly reduce deforestation (SCI-137) | Counterfactual matching recipes, project boundary registries, and tenure review |
| Protected and conserved areas | Only 4.78% of global land is covered by protected areas with assessed management effectiveness (SCI-136) | Alignment with management-effectiveness protocols |
| Dryland water accounting | 3.2 billion people live in agricultural areas with high or very high water shortages or scarcity (SCI-139) | Evapotranspiration and irrigation-mask recipes |
| Pastoral drought assessment | Drylands host 69% of global livestock production (SCI-129) | Local-language source corpora, seasonal rainfall terciles, and stratified field protocols |
