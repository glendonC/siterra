# Comparable places

Why a case can show other places where something similar was studied, and the rules that keep those places from being read as evidence about this valley. Sources and the full assessment: [data science research](../../research/science/analytics.md) and the [science source register](../../research/science/source-register.md) (SCI-87 to SCI-111).

## Why

Restoration practice builds a reference model from several comparable sites plus local knowledge (the Society for Ecological Restoration's international standards, SCI-87). A program manager deciding on next steps reasonably asks: has anything like this been tried in comparable places, and under what conditions? Showing those places gives the intervention comparison and the field plan concrete designs to learn from, such as exclosure plots and herd tracking near settlements.

## Why it needs rules

Comparing with a reference does not show cause; that needs a before-after-control-impact design (SCI-88 to SCI-91). Adjacent valleys differ through several factors at once: Aït Bouguemez and the neighbouring Aït Bou Oulli valley followed very different forest trajectories (SCI-102). A comparable place is therefore context by analogy, never a control and never a forecast for this valley.

## Rules

1. Claim kind is always published context with an analogy scope; never an observation, model estimate or verified outcome.
2. Every card shows place and scale, what was studied and how (a design label from a fixed vocabulary), the period, the finding with a verified excerpt, similarities and differences on named dimensions (elevation, climate, vegetation, livestock, governance, access, scale, period), the transfer limits, and what the place cannot tell us about Aït Bouguemez. The last two are required.
3. No numeric transfer: a comparable place's numbers appear only inside its card, attributed to the source. The numeric provenance check rejects them anywhere else.
4. A comparable place may support an intervention as feasibility context or inform a field requirement. It cannot change a hypothesis status without at least one observation from the case.
5. Order is fixed and explained (same mountain range first, then other Moroccan mountains, then syntheses). No similarity scores, no ranking.
6. Locations at publication-locator precision only.

## Verified candidates

| Place | What was studied | Main finding | Why transfer is limited |
|---|---|---|---|
| Southern side of the Central High Atlas (SCI-93 to SCI-95) | Browsed and unbrowsed plots 2004 to 2009; GPS-collared village goat herds | Browsing reduced biomass gains of *Artemisia herba-alba*; grazing concentrated within about 250 to 500 m of settlements | Likely drier side of the massif; one exclosure; dwarf-shrub steppe |
| Yagour agdal, Mesioua (SCI-96, SCI-23, SCI-24) | Ethnography and household economics | About a three-month spring herding ban; up to 18% of users' agropastoral income through fodder | Economic, not ecological; different tribe and institution |
| Oukaimeden plateau (SCI-98 to SCI-101) | Aerial-photo history, floristic surveys, ethnoarchaeology, pollen record | Small, species-rich wet pastures altered by a 1970s dam and drains; customary access rules coexist with tourism | Wet habitat; ski and tourism infrastructure |
| Aït Bou Oulli, the neighbouring valley (SCI-102) | Paired-valley forest history from aerial documents | Very different forest trajectories in two adjacent valleys | Forest, not pasture; shows why a neighbour is not a control |
| Ifrane National Park, Middle Atlas (SCI-103) | Ten-year closure compared with a grazed area | Much higher phytomass and diversity in the closed area | One pair, no before data, more humid forest rangeland, full closure |
| Central High Atlas mountain oases (SCI-104) | 128 interviews | Succession and labour problems, out-migration, drought, blocked transhumance routes | Livelihood context only |
| Ouled Dlim, Marrakech province (SCI-109) | Shrub plantations against adjacent rangeland, field-calibrated imagery | Plantations held more dry biomass; early grazing undermined several | Lowland plain; planting, not rest |
| Holistic Planned Grazing meta-analysis (SCI-110) | Studies 1972 to 2016 | No difference from continuous grazing on cover, biomass or animal gain | Global, mostly non-mountain |
| Livestock exclusion meta-analysis (SCI-111) | 109 studies | Exclusion raised animal abundance and diversity | Global; exclusion differs from seasonal rest |

A local calibration study in Aït Bouguemez and Midelt (SCI-106) is not a management analogue: it shows that the best drone index explained 42% of biomass variance and that bare soil hides biomass below about 800 kg per hectare, which confirms that satellite greenness cannot stand in for biomass here.

## Priority

P0: three or four of these sources join the case corpus, so source research produces published-context evidence with "Analogy:" scope notes and transfer limits, without a schema change. P1: a "Comparable places" view on a regional map and a structured `AnalogueAssessment` record ([data contracts](../reference/data-contracts.md)).
